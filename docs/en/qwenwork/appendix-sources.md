# Sources and Further Reading

## Plain-Language Orientation

When you quote a product fact or a case conclusion from this Blue Book, the worst outcome is a source you cannot explain and a boundary that does not line up. This appendix lists every public source, R1–R15, with the access date and the verification boundary attached, so you can cite it in a report or a decision without attributing it to the wrong place. Below is when you open it, how to read each entry, and what to do when a link dies.

## Who This Appendix Is For and When to Open It

Product interfaces, pricing, plans, model lists, available regions, and connector scope change quickly; the official pages at time of use prevail. When you quote pricing or connector scope from R2, R3, or R12, copying the data from the access date may no longer match the current page. When you see an entry marked "accessed," read it as a snapshot of that date and check the official page again before you act on it.

## How to Read Each Entry and What Misreading Costs You

Read the type first: R1 is a methodological reference (MIT licensed), R2–R9 and R12–R13 are official pages, R10–R11 are the Alibaba knowledge base. Treat a community knowledge base as an official commitment and you overestimate product capability; miss the "accessed" date and you take an old snapshot for the current state. Each entry's limitations are written into its text—read them before citing, and don't just copy the title.

Compatibility numbering entry points: [R14](#r14), [R15](#r15). These two are compatibility redirects for older numbering; when the body cites R14 or R15, they actually point to R8 and R4—do not treat them as two independent sources.

## FAQ

**When citing this Blue Book, which source should you attribute to?**
Attribute to the R entry matching the bracketed number in the body—for example, R3 for the product introduction and R4 for connectors. Don't write up the community knowledge base entries R10 and R11 as official commitments; they serve to explain positioning, and actual capability is governed by account testing and the official pages.

**The body cites R14 or R15. Where do you find the original?**
R14 and R15 are compatibility redirects pointing to R8 (Skills) and R4 (Connectors) respectively; they are not independent sources. Cite R8 or R4 directly and don't let the numbering look like two extra sources.

**A link will not open or the page has been redesigned. What do you do?**
Read the entry's "accessed" date as the snapshot of that moment, then open the official page to check the current version. If pricing, plans, or connector scope no longer match the access date, the page at time of use prevails—and note the date you re-checked at the point of citation.

**When you draw a case conclusion, what does R11 give you and what does it not?**
R11 lets you verify case names, task tags, role tags, and public artifact links; most of its entries disclose neither customer names nor quantified outcomes. Don't reverse-engineer "effect data" from the case library—where nothing is publicly quantified, write only as far as "the form is demonstrable."
