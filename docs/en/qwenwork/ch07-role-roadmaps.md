# Role Roadmaps: Pick Real Tasks with the Unified Scenario Card

## 30-Second Conclusion

<span id="claim-role-scenario-card-01" data-claim-id="claim-role-scenario-card-01"></span>This book recommends: register role-level AI scenarios uniformly with ten fields—business problem, role, inputs, current steps, deliverable, acceptance, forbidden actions, metrics, risks, baseline—proving one task works before combining role workflows.

- A job title cannot substitute for a specific business problem.
- Metrics must be compared against the manual baseline, watching quality and risk at the same time.
- Scenario scoring, sample requirements, and stage gates are defined only by Chapter 10.

## A Scenario You Might Hit

You work in HR, sales, product, finance/legal, or content/marketing and want an AI task that truly lands, rather than a generic "give this role an assistant." Input examples can be a recruiting weekly ledger, customer visit materials, project risk records, contracts and detail, or verified sources and brand guidelines; write it up as a task card first, then pick a route.

## What You End Up With

The final product is a role scenario card, structured as: business problem, role, inputs, current steps, deliverable, acceptance, forbidden actions, metrics, risks, baseline; the card should also mark the corresponding task page, the result's owner, and the human confirmer.

## Follow Along

1. First pick a task within the role that is high-frequency, has obtainable inputs, a human-judgeable result, and reversible failure; do not substitute the job title for the problem.
2. Fill in the five fields per the [task delivery protocol](/en/qwenwork/ch02-task-delivery-protocol), then add the role, forbidden actions, metrics, and baseline.
3. **Mid-point checkpoint:** have the business owner confirm the deliverable and acceptance, the data or system owner confirm authorization, and the content or professional reviewer confirm quality boundaries.
4. Start trial runs from the corresponding task pages: delivery in [The New Delivery Standard](/en/qwenwork/ch01-delivery-standard), office artifacts in [Office Delivery](/en/qwenwork/ch06-office-delivery), research in [Research and the Evidence Chain](/en/qwenwork/ch08-research-evidence-chain).

## Case Reference

For role routes, you can reference the research and collateral tasks in [Pisen: Competitive Research and Product Collateral Production](/en/qwenwork/case-pisen), or the R&D and payroll tasks in [Youkela: Product R&D and Attendance-Based Payroll](/en/qwenwork/case-youkela); these cases cannot replace your own baselines, permissions, and professional confirmations.

## Post-Task Check

Check item by item whether another colleague can restate the scenario card as "which inputs, which artifact, pass under which conditions, which actions are forbidden"; and confirm there is at least one quality metric and one manual baseline. If they cannot restate it, send the card back for completion.

## Watch Out

If the scenario has only role slogans with no obtainable inputs, or acceptance depends on high-consequence judgments such as hiring, payment, legal opinions, roadmap commitments, or external publishing, keep the existing manual process, stop the automation trial, switch to manual organization or read-only analysis, and pick the task only after business and professional owners confirm.

## Why It Matters

"Give HR an assistant" or "build a smart agent for sales" cannot be accepted directly. The role scope is too large, and data permissions and consequences differ; one successful material-organization task cannot prove that hiring decisions, quotes, or publishing actions can be automated.

The unified scenario card compresses role needs into comparable business units. Enterprise AI owners can pick pilots by real inputs, current steps, and risk; business owners can confirm deliverables and forbidden actions; operations teams can collect same-basis baselines.

## Method or Model

The scenario card's ten fields keep the same meaning across all roles; the five cards below are starter examples, not fixed best practice.

### HR: Recruiting Weekly Status Organization

| Field | Scenario content |
|---|---|
| Business problem | Before the recruiting weekly, quickly identify blocked positions and missing feedback |
| Role | Recruiting lead, recruiter, hiring manager |
| Inputs | Authorized position ledger, interview feedback, onboarding checklist, last week's version |
| Current steps | Export, merge, chase missing items, organize status, prepare the weekly |
| Deliverable | An editable status table of position—stage—blocker—owner—next step |
| Acceptance | Every status traces to original feedback; missing items listed separately; owners and deadlines clear |
| Forbidden actions | No automatic hiring, rejection, or candidate evaluation; no spreading personal information to unauthorized parties |
| Metrics | Preparation time, feedback completeness, status mismatch rate, manual-edit volume |
| Risks | Candidate privacy, feedback bias, same-name mismatches, overwriting old status |
| Baseline | Record current manual prep time, missing feedback, rejections, and correction samples |

### Sales and Business: Customer Visit Prep and Follow-Up

| Field | Scenario content |
|---|---|
| Business problem | Pre-meeting information is scattered; post-meeting actions and commitments slip through |
| Role | Account manager, presales, sales owner |
| Inputs | Public information, authorized CRM records, past communications, product materials |
| Current steps | Collect, verify, build an outline, take meeting notes, draft follow-up |
| Deliverable | One-page prep sheet, facts and to-confirm questions, email draft, to-write list |
| Acceptance | Facts labeled with source and date; commitments, quotes, owners, and deadlines confirmed |
| Forbidden actions | No automatic quoting, committing, sending, or editing customer master records |
| Metrics | Prep time, fact error rate, action omission rate, CRM suggestion adoption rate |
| Risks | Unauthorized customer data, stale information, fabricated commitments, wrong recipients |
| Baseline | Collect current prep time, post-meeting omissions, bounced mail, and record corrections |

### Product and Project: Project Risk Ledger

| Field | Scenario content |
|---|---|
| Business problem | Changes across requirements, weeklies, and defects don't roll up into decision requests in time |
| Role | Product manager, project manager, R&D lead |
| Inputs | Requirement docs, project plan, weekly notes, defects, and change logs |
| Current steps | Align versions, extract changes, judge impact, assign ownership, request decisions |
| Deliverable | Risk ledger, change summary, pending decisions, draft release notes |
| Acceptance | Risks have evidence, owner, deadline, and mitigation; decisions kept separate from suggestions |
| Forbidden actions | No automatic priority changes, defect closing, release-date commitments, or roadmap overwrites |
| Metrics | Roll-up time, risk omission rate, decision wait time, change traceability |
| Risks | Mixed versions, ownership mismatches, unconfirmed status written as decisions |
| Baseline | Record existing roll-up steps, omissions, wait times, and post-meeting revisions |

### Finance and Legal: High-Consequence Material Verification

| Field | Scenario content |
|---|---|
| Business problem | Large volumes of detail and clauses need anomaly location, but final judgment must be borne by professionals |
| Role | Finance staff, legal staff, business owner |
| Inputs | Authorized detail, statements, contract versions, rules, professional templates |
| Current steps | Reconciliation or clause extraction, anomaly location, version comparison, build review lists |
| Deliverable | Finance reconciliation table or clause location table, anomaly list, version diffs, to-confirm items |
| Acceptance | Amounts, dates, formulas, clause locations, and versions traceable; professionals sign off conclusions |
| Forbidden actions | No automatic bookkeeping, payments, master-data edits, final legal opinions, or contract signing |
| Metrics | Verification time, anomaly detection rate, false positive rate, manual edits, rejections |
| Risks | Amount or version errors, permission overreach, hints mistaken for professional conclusions |
| Baseline | Save manual verification time, known anomalies, missed detections, false positives, review records |

### Content and Marketing: An Evidence-Based Content Pipeline

| Field | Scenario content |
|---|---|
| Business problem | Topic selection, assets, review, publishing, and retrospectives are scattered; fact and brand risk is hard to control |
| Role | Content operations, marketing, brand and design owners |
| Inputs | Verified sources, topic pool, brand guidelines, authorized assets, channel requirements |
| Current steps | Research, topic selection, drafting, design, fact review, preview, publish, retrospective |
| Deliverable | Topic evidence table, content draft, asset list, publish preview, retrospective record |
| Acceptance | Facts have sources and dates; brand, copyright, links, and channel format pass checks |
| Forbidden actions | No unauthorized assets, no fabricated outcomes or customers, no external publishing without confirmation |
| Metrics | Production time, fact rejection rate, brand issues, publishing errors, reuse rate |
| Risks | Copyright, misleading wording, sensitive information, expired links, wrong channels |
| Baseline | Collect current cycle time, review edits, publishing incidents, asset reuse |

## Cases and Evidence

The five cards come from community organization of existing role tasks, using no unauthorized customer names or effect numbers. They demonstrate how to fill in a scenario card, not that these scenarios have the same priority in every organization.

Metrics must be observed in pairs, efficiency alongside quality. For example, when prep time drops, also check whether fact errors, manual edits, and risk incidents rose. Results without a manual baseline or measured on a different basis cannot be compared directly.

## Enterprise Actions

Pick only one candidate task per role:

1. The business owner fills in the business problem, deliverable, acceptance, and forbidden actions.
2. The user records current steps, input locations, and the manual baseline.
3. The data or system owner verifies authorization scope and high-risk actions.
4. The content reviewer defines quality metrics and rejection reasons.
5. Submit the completed scenario card to the Chapter 10 stage-gate review; this chapter does no scoring or thresholds of its own.

Stop signals include: the scenario can only be described by job title; no obtainable inputs; deliverable not acceptable; forbidden actions unclear; metrics record speed only; the baseline uses a different basis or keeps only success samples.

## Boundaries and Next Steps

Scenario cards are not role performance reviews, and AI output cannot replace decisions on hiring, payment, legal opinions, roadmap commitments, or external publishing. Organizations should add stricter rules by data sensitivity and professional consequence.

This chapter defines role scenarios, not scoring and promotion gates. After the scenario cards are done, the team still needs a research and evidence method, to avoid writing impressions into business facts.
