# Scenario Lookup and Scoring Sheet

## Plain-Language Orientation

When you work on a role roadmap or a pilot roadmap you keep filling in the scenario card and the scoring sheet, but the field definitions live in two different chapters and you end up flipping back and forth. This appendix gathers the 10 scenario-card fields from Chapter 7 and the 7 scoring dimensions from Chapter 10 into copyable blanks—print them or copy them out and fill them in directly. Below is what this is for, when to open it, how to fill each field, and what goes wrong if you fill it in badly.

## Who This Appendix Is For and When to Open It

This appendix provides fill-in copies only and establishes no new scenario standards. The scenario card fields and their meanings have [Chapter 7 Role Roadmaps](/en/qwenwork/ch07-role-roadmaps) as their sole source; candidate scenario scoring, evidence requirements, and usage boundaries have the [Chapter 10 pilot roadmap](/en/qwenwork/ch10-pilot-roadmap) as their sole source.

If you are filling in a scenario card for the first time, read the definitions of the 10 fields in Chapter 7 first, then come back and copy the field names and their order. Invent your own fields and the basis will no longer match Chapter 7, and the later pilot stage gates will have nothing to check against. The scoring sheet takes scores and their basis only; weighting and thresholds stay in Chapter 10—do not define anchors on this page.

## How to Pick Each Copy and What Happens If You Fill It Wrong

Fill the scenario card in the given order of the 10 fields from Chapter 7; do not add or drop fields. Leaving out forbidden actions or risks removes the circuit breaker you need during the pilot. Score the 7 dimensions on the Chapter 10 scale; scores only order the validation sequence and are not a conclusion about outcomes. The two tables below are kept as they are—just copy the field names and fill them in.

## FAQ

**You are filling in a scenario card for the first time. Which chapter do you start with?**
Start with [Chapter 7 Role Roadmaps](/en/qwenwork/ch07-role-roadmaps) and work through the definitions and meaning of the 10 fields, then come back and copy the field names and order. Fill it in by feel and your field basis will not match the authoritative chapter, and the pilot stage gates later will have nothing to check against.

**What happens if you leave out the forbidden actions or risks fields?**
Those two fields are the pilot's circuit breaker. Without them, when an unauthorized action or a business incident occurs during a run, you have no explicit pre-agreed boundary to compare against and no way to judge whether to stop. Don't skip a single one of the ten fields.

**Can you define your own score anchors on this page?**
No. Score anchors, weighting, and thresholds belong to the [Chapter 10 pilot roadmap](/en/qwenwork/ch10-pilot-roadmap); this page only gives you copyable blanks. Define your own anchors and scores stop being comparable across scenarios, and the validation order you derive is distorted.

**After filling in the copies, where do you go next to check them?**
Go back to Chapter 10 to check the current stage gates and approval records, and confirm that the scores were used only to order validation and not read as a conclusion about outcomes. For the public status of case leads, check inclusion and boundaries in the [case source map](/en/qwenwork/appendix-case-source-map).
