# You Walked Away From Your Desk. Does the Running Task Just Stop?

You just handed your desktop a job, and then your boss calls you into a meeting—or you're on the subway wondering how far that summary has gotten. Your laptop is on the desk and you are somewhere else entirely, and you can't exactly carry it around. Some tasks run for ten-plus minutes; sitting there waiting is its own kind of miserable.

This is when Doubao Work's "remote control from phone" fits. It's built for exactly this: you've stepped away from the computer but want the job to keep going. You check progress from outside, add one more instruction, and the machine actually doing the work is still your computer. Step out for a bit and you can direct your desktop from the back of a taxi, without sitting in front of the screen.

## Turning It On

First, make sure your phone and your computer are signed in to the **same Doubao account**. Don't skip this—wrong account and your phone won't find your computer at all.

Then, on the computer, open "Remote Control from Phone" and switch on "Allow control by devices signed in to the same account." Only then is your phone allowed to connect.

For jobs that run a long time, also turn on "**Keep this device awake**." With it on, your computer won't kill the task by locking its screen while you go do something else. It cuts both ways, though—turn it off when you're done instead of leaving it on.

After that, open a work task in the Doubao mobile app and pick the computer that shows as online.

On your first connection, send a **read-only test** before you let it do anything real. The point is to confirm your phone is connected to your computer and not someone else's machine on a shared account:

```text
Tell me the name of the most recent file in the Downloads folder.
```

If you recognize the file name it reports back, you're connected to the right one.

## When It's Worth the Most

- Checking how far a long task already running on your computer has gotten;
- Having Doubao dig through a folder for candidate files and send a summary back;
- Keeping local materials, spreadsheets, or project files organized without going back to the desk;
- Unblocking a task that's stalled waiting for your confirmation, straight from your phone;
- Getting progress notifications, then returning to the computer to check the finished work when you're free.

## A Real Moment

You suddenly need to revise a summary, but you're away from your desk: open Doubao Work on your phone, connect to your own computer, have Doubao locate that summary on the machine first, then make the edits, and send it straight back to a colleague via Feishu—without touching the computer once, you watch it get done from your phone.

## FAQ

**My phone won't connect to my computer. Where's it most likely stuck?**
Check the account first: are your phone and computer signed in to the same Doubao account? Then check whether "Allow control by devices signed in to the same account" is switched on in the desktop app. If either one is off, your phone simply won't see your computer.

**Can I just leave "Keep this device awake" on all the time?**
Better not. Turn it on only when a job will run long and you need to walk away, so a lock screen doesn't cut it off. Turn it off once the work is done—don't leave it hanging there.

**Why send a read-only test on the first connection?**
To confirm your phone is really on your computer, not another machine under the same account name. A read-only test changes nothing; it asks for one file name, which is both safe and easy to recognize. Once you're sure, send real work.

**What kind of instructions can I give from my phone?**
The same ones you'd give at the computer: check progress, add a requirement, give a confirmation, look for a file in a specific folder. Execution still happens on that computer—your phone is just how you watch and steer it while you're out.

**Can I do things like sending a file through Feishu from my phone?**
Yes. Like the moment above: you're out, you have Doubao fix the summary and send it straight back to a colleague through Feishu, and you never touch the computer. That works only if the desktop side already has a channel like Feishu connected.
